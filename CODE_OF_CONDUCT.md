# Code of Conduct

## The standard

Be straightforward and be kind. Assume the person on the other side is trying to do good work with
incomplete information, because that is usually true.

Unacceptable: harassment, personal attacks, demeaning or discriminatory comments, publishing
someone's private information, and sustained disruption of discussion. Disagreement is not
disruption — disagreeing with a technical decision, loudly, with reasons, is welcome here.

## Technical honesty is part of this

This project's rules about evidence apply to how we talk to each other, not just to what we commit:

- Do not claim verification you did not perform. In a review, in an issue, in a benchmark comment.
- If you are wrong, say so plainly and move on. Nobody is owed a defence of a mistaken claim, and
  admitting one costs nothing here.
- Do not present a guess as a measurement. "I think" and "I measured" are different sentences.

Getting a correction is not an insult, and issuing one is not an attack.

## Scope

Applies in this repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages — and anywhere
someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Report a concern through **GitHub's private vulnerability reporting** on this repository's Security
tab, the same private channel [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) uses. It reaches the maintainers and
nobody else. Please do not open a public issue for a conduct concern.

Reports are read by the maintainers, who will respond as promptly as they can and keep the reporter's
identity confidential. Possible responses range from a private word to removing content to blocking
an account, depending on severity and pattern.

Maintainers are held to this document too. A maintainer breaching it is a more serious problem than a
first-time contributor doing so, not a lesser one.

## Attribution

Written for this project rather than adopted wholesale, so that every clause is one the maintainers
actually intend to enforce. It is shorter than the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/)
and deliberately so — an unenforced policy is worse than a short one that is.
